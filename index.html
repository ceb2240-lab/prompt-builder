PREPARE Prompt Builder

Build a strong prompt one step at a time. The first four steps (PREP) build your prompt; the last three (ARE) make it even better.

What do you want the AI to do?

The Prompt is your core request. Start with a clear question or instruction that sets the stage for everything that follows.

Ask yourself

  • What’s the single task? (write, plan, summarise, explain, compare…)
  • What’s the topic or material it’s about?
  • What will you do with the result?
One or two sentences. Start with a verb.
The goal behind the task helps the AI make better choices.
See a weak vs. strong prompt
WeakAI in education.
StrongerWrite a summary about the latest AI trends in education, so I can share key takeaways with my department.

Who should the AI be?

Give the AI a Role and outline the context. The role sets the knowledge and perspective it brings; the context tells it about the situation it’s working in.

Ask yourself

  • Whose expertise would you hire for this task?
  • How experienced are they, and where do they work?
  • What does the AI need to know about your situation?
Describe a person, not just a job title.
Methods, standards or experience the role should use.
Who’s involved, what’s happened so far, any constraints.
See a weak vs. strong role
WeakAct as an expert.
StrongerYou’re an education expert analysing AI trends. My department is deciding whether to adopt AI tools next year, and most colleagues are new to AI.

Spell out the details

Be Explicit to avoid misunderstandings. List exactly what the response must include, in what order, and what to leave out.

Ask yourself

  • What must the response include?
  • Is there an order or structure it should follow?
  • What would make me reject the answer?
Specific instructions One instruction per line. Aim for at least two.
Things you don’t want: jargon, clichés, certain topics.
See weak vs. strong instructions
WeakMake it good and thorough.
StrongerMention how AI can contribute to personalised learning. Include one benefit and one risk for each trend. End with three questions for discussion.

Shape the output

Set clear Parameters, such as the format, length, tone of voice and audience. This is how you get something you can use straight away.

Ask yourself

  • What should the finished thing look like?
  • How long is too long?
  • Who will read it, and how should it sound?
Tone Pick any that fit.
One per line: language, spelling, citations, things to include at the end.
Booster

Invite the AI to ask questions

Ask the AI to ask you clarifying questions before it continues. Its questions often reveal details you forgot to include, which leads to a better first answer.

Ask yourself

  • Is anything about the task open to interpretation?
  • Would a human expert want to check something with you first?
  • How many questions do you have time to answer?
Choose a starter or write your own.
In the workshopWhen the AI replies with questions, answer them in the same chat. It will then use your answers to complete the task.
Booster

Ask the AI to rate its work

Rate asks the AI to score its own output and say what could be improved. It’s a quick way to surface weak spots you can follow up on.

Ask yourself

  • What does “good” look like for this task?
  • Which criteria matter most: accuracy, clarity, tone, completeness?
  • Should it just tell you what to improve, or improve it too?
Choose a starter or write your own.
Keep in mindA high self-rating isn’t proof of quality. AI can be overconfident about its own work, so you still need to check the result yourself.
Booster

Add an emotional cue

Emotion adds a short line that signals the task matters or encourages care. Some research has found cues like this can improve the quality of responses.

Ask yourself

  • Why does this task matter to you or to others?
  • Would a word of encouragement fit the tone of your prompt?
Choose a starter or write your own.
Try it as an experimentThe evidence here is mixed, and newer AI models tend to respond more to clear instructions than to encouragement. Run your prompt with and without this line and compare the results.

Your finished prompt

Check it reads well, then copy it into your AI tool of choice.

Before you paste this into an AI toolMake sure it holds nothing confidential, and know how you’ll verify and disclose the result.

    After you send it

    • Read the response critically: is it accurate, complete and right for your audience?
    • If it misses, don’t start over. Reply with what to change, and note which PREPARE step was too thin.
    • Save prompts that work well and reuse them as templates.

    Use AI responsibly

    A well-built prompt is only half the job. Keep these in mind every time you use AI.

    • Keep confidential information outDon’t paste student records or grades (protected under FERPA), unpublished research, or manuscripts and proposals you’re reviewing into public AI tools. Use your institution’s approved tools instead.
    • Verify before you use itAI can invent citations and state wrong facts with confidence. Check every reference and key claim against the original source.
    • Be open about your useFollow the AI disclosure policies of your institution, journal or funder, and let students know how you use AI in your teaching.
    • Check your course’s AI policyBefore using AI for a class, read the AI statement in the course syllabus and check with your faculty member. Expectations can differ from one course to the next.
    • Own the final resultAI is an assistant, not an author. You’re responsible for anything you share, submit or publish.

    AI policies can change quickly. Be sure to check in on your institution’s or workplace’s guidance.
